# immune-aging

Aging remodels the human immune system, yet its temporal dynamics, cellular architecture, and modifiable accelerators remain incompletely defined. Here, we performed deep immunophenotyping and transcriptomic profiling in 1,001 healthy adults to construct a high-resolution map of immune aging across adulthood. We identify a previously unrecognized inflection point in the late thirties marked by coordinated immune-cell activation and differentiation. Integrating cellular and molecular signatures, we developed an immune aging clock that quantifies inter-individual immune age acceleration and delineates features of accelerated immune aging at cellular and transcriptomic resolution. Obesity and unfavorable lifestyle showed additive associations with immune age acceleration, with mediation analyses implicating metabolic stress responses and T cell differentiation pathways. Together, these findings define the architecture of human immune aging, reveal a midlife immune remodeling transition, and nominate stress-responsive pathways linking metabolic and behavioral states to immune aging, providing a systems-level framework to guide future studies of modifiable contributors to immunosenescence.
